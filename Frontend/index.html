02 / UNDERSTAND
TWIN LAB

Follow the calculation chain used by the prototype. The model is deterministic: the same inputs produce the same result.

01

Temperature → Viscosity

Heavy-oil viscosity falls as temperature increases. API gravity provides an adjustment to the reference viscosity.

REDUCED-ORDER HEAVY-OIL RELATIONSHIP
μ(T) = μref × exp[ B(1/T − 1/Tref) ] × F(API)
02

Steam → Heat

Available steam rate, steam temperature and steam quality determine the effective thermal input to the well.

STEAM ENERGY × EFFECTIVE HEAT-TRANSFER FACTOR
Qsteam = Msteam × Hsteam × ηthermal
03

Heat → Temperature

The injected thermal energy is translated into an estimated reservoir/oil temperature response using an effective thermal capacity.

LUMPED THERMAL CAPACITY MODEL
ΔT = Qeffective / Cthermal
04

Mobility Improvement

Lower viscosity increases the relative mobility of the heavy oil and improves the production response of the reduced-order reservoir model.

VISCOSITY-BASED MOBILITY RESPONSE
Mobility Gain ∝ (μbefore / μafter)n
05

Steam Schedule

The optimizer evaluates candidate steam rates and injection durations rather than returning a random value.

CANDIDATE SEARCH / REDUCED-ORDER OPTIMIZATION
Mtotal = Mrate × tinjection
06

Pressure Response

Injection pressure is incorporated as a pressure-effect factor in the thermal/production response model.

SIMPLIFIED PRESSURE FACTOR
fP = clamp( Pinj / Preference, Pmin, Pmax )
07

Soak → Cooling

After injection, the model estimates cooling during the soak period before pump restart.

TIME-DEPENDENT THERMAL DECLINE
Trestart = Theated − rsoak × tsoak
08

Production Forecast

Production declines from the predicted stimulated condition toward the production cut-off.

REDUCED-ORDER PRODUCTION DECLINE MODEL
q(t) = qstimulated × decline(t)
09

SRP Capacity

Pump capacity is estimated from stroke, strokes per minute and pump efficiency.

SIMPLIFIED PUMP CAPACITY RELATIONSHIP
Qpump ∝ Stroke × SPM × ηpump
10

Rod-Load Risk

Current rod load, pump efficiency, viscosity and operating speed contribute to the surface risk estimate.

RULE-BASED SRP RISK PROTOTYPE
Risk = f( Rod Load, SPM, Stroke, μ, ηpump )
11

Integrated Objective

CSS and SRP are evaluated together instead of treating steam stimulation and artificial lift as separate optimization problems.

PROTOTYPE MULTI-OBJECTIVE SCORE
Score = f( Production Gain, Energy Index, Rod Risk )
MODEL NOTE — These equations describe the reduced-order prototype currently implemented in OilTwin. They are engineering approximations, not a commercial reservoir simulator and not field-calibrated operating correlations.

What The Inputs Mean

Temperature controls the viscosity estimate. Pressure and API describe the reservoir/oil state. Steam temperature, quality and rate determine thermal input. SPM, stroke, pump efficiency and rod load describe the artificial lift condition.

Reading The Result

The recommended schedule combines CSS injection, soak and pump restart with the post-stimulation SRP setting. Production increase is relative to the current production supplied to the model.

Try An Experiment

Change only one variable and run the twin again. For example, reduce reservoir temperature and observe the viscosity and production response. Then increase the available steam rate and compare the resulting schedule.

Important Limitation

This demonstration does not replace a reservoir simulator, wellbore model, commercial pump simulator or field engineering review. The current well dataset is representative/synthetic. Actual deployment would require field history, calibration, equipment constraints and engineering validation.
